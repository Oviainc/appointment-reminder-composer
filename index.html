Every missed slot in one place, grouped by the reason the client gave — forgot, work, travel, plans changed, or another reason — with the no-reason count always shown alongside: how many slots were missed for each reason, how many were no-shows and how many cancellations, what share of all missed slots each reason carries, and how many of its slots were refilled — so the pattern by cause is visible too, not only the pattern by client, by service, by weekday, by time of day, or by lead time. A reason recorded before the short list existed keeps the client’s own words: one that matches the list counts under it, and any other counts as another reason given, never re-labelled.

Reminder composer

Pick a saved appointment, choose a step, and copy a ready-to-send message in the appointment language. The sequence that works: a confirmation the moment the appointment is booked, a check-in one week before for a booking made well ahead, a reminder 48 hours before, a reminder 24 hours before, a short one on the morning of the appointment, and one on the way. The one-week check-in is the mid-window step for a distant booking: a week before the appointment, it brings the booking back to mind and asks the client to confirm it still works, or to move it while a full week remains to refill the slot — and the suggested next step surfaces it exactly in that week, after the confirmation, before the 48-hour reminder takes over. The 48-hour reminder is the rescheduling window: it asks the client to confirm, cancel, or move while there is still time to offer the slot to someone else, and the suggested next step surfaces it exactly in that window — after the confirmation, before the 24-hour reminder takes over. When someone still does not show, send the first follow-up soon — while the slot is still fresh — and a second one two days later offering a new time. When a client comes, the visit does not end at the door: a post-visit note thanks them, invites them to book the next appointment, and asks for a few words about their visit — the attended outcome suggests it first, and it is logged on the appointment like every other step. Every message we compose is logged on the appointment, so we always know what was sent and when. The appointments list suggests the next step for every booking — worked out from its time, the messages already logged, and its recorded outcome — and picking an appointment here pre-selects that step, so what to send next is decided before the moment arrives. The outcome buttons on each appointment (attended, no-show, cancelled) are part of the record too: they drive the no-show rate above, the per-client history, and which follow-up the composer suggests next. Recording a no-show or a cancellation asks for the reason the client gave, picked from a short list — forgot, work, travel, plans changed, or another reason — or recorded as none given when the client said nothing, and the reason shows on the appointment; the By reason view counts every missed slot by that reason, with the no-reason count always shown, so the biggest cause in the book tells us which fix to try first; a missed slot that went to someone else, from the waitlist or a walk-in, is marked refilled on the appointment, and the dashboard counts the slots refilled beside the no-show rate, so the record shows what was recovered, not only what was lost. When a client replies that they are coming, the Confirmed button on their appointment records it, with the date: a confirmed badge shows on the booking, the suggested next step stops treating it as unanswered, and the per-client history counts the confirmation — so a client who answered is never chased like one who stayed silent. Rescheduling an appointment clears its confirmation, because the new time needs a fresh answer. The dashboard keeps the call list: every upcoming booking that has not confirmed yet sits under Still to confirm, with its contact and whether the confirmation was sent, and the dashboard counts how many upcoming bookings are confirmed ahead — so the day-before calls go only to the clients who have not answered, the short list, not the whole book. For a client whose record reaches three no-shows, the suggested next step on a new booking is the deposit request — a ready-made message in the client's language explaining that a deposit now holds their slot, that it comes off the bill in full when they come, and that it only stays with the business if the appointment is missed again without notice; once that request is logged, the normal confirmation rhythm resumes. When a cancellation policy is saved for the business, it travels with the confirmation, the 48-hour reminder, and the 24-hour reminder, under a label in the appointment language — stated in the message itself, not remembered from an earlier conversation. When a booking moves, the rescheduled message states both the old and the new time and asks the client to confirm the new slot, and it is suggested first until it is sent; when a booking is cancelled, the cancellation message closes the loop and invites the client to book again, so a moved or cancelled booking never leaves the client guessing at the old time. Every confirmation, check-in, and reminder also states the time reserved for the appointment — the duration saved with the booking, in the appointment language — so the client sees the concrete slot being held for them, not only its start time. When the message reads right, send it straight from here by email or WhatsApp, download a calendar file (.ics) the client can add in one tap, or copy the message into any other channel.
